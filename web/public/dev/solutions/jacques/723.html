Findings so far

  1. The strongest preserved route is alive at tick 3808 with Chip at (23,0), 53 chips collected and only (31,0) remaining. All 3,809 steps, the initial state and terminal result match native Tile World MS exactly. An intermediate 52-chip checkpoint at tick 3172 is also separately native-verified.
  2. The left dead-end chip at (2,12) must be collected before pushing the block from (2,9) down the column. The older 40-chip route leaves that block at (2,11), where it seals the uncollected chip. The new prefix visits (2,12) and (2,14) from the outside before entering the block enclosure.
  3. The first block gate remains reversible: park its returning block at (21,0), use the loop beneath the ice to reach (22,0), and return through (22,2), (21,2) and (20,2).
  4. The new route collects (27,4) in the teleporter room, then visits the glider-column side rooms before leaving through the block at (26,9). With water boots, push the block at (23,12) west across its ice, collect the upper dead-end (23,7), and visit (16,12), (15,13) and (18,14). It also collects (29,5) before the central passage is opened.
  5. The central bomb at (8,16) is cleared with a freshly cloned block extracted south from (11,13) and pushed west along row 15. The new route additionally collects the previously untouched chip at (10,14).
  6. All sixteen chips beneath the lower ice-grid blocks are collected by pushing west on a tick congruent to 2 modulo 4 and retreating east at the next eligible input poll.
  7. The right-hand room is solved constructively. Reach (31,22) from below through (31,23). Transport two lower-left stock blocks east through row 28, push them south at x=26, and then east along cleared rows 30 and 31 into their bombs; collect (31,30) and (31,31).
  8. After clearing the right-hand room, the route returns through the outer corridor and teleporter to the upper gate. A 99-tick wait permits safe passage past the changed fireball timing. Push the gate block east to collect (24,0), then retreat west before it returns; the preserved checkpoint is alive on the accessible side of the gate.
  9. The second upper gate remains unresolved. Its block starts over (24,0), slides across (25..27,0), and returns from the wall at (28,0). Removing gateway (24,0) leaves a nine-cell final pocket containing (31,0); the directed static geometry has no ordinary exit to the lower board. North-facing thin walls prevent a perpendicular push from row 1 into the ice.
  10. Native movement checks reject the thin-wall departure before attempting a block push, including moves selected by a mouse goal. A stationary block at (24,0) cannot be pushed away from inside the final pocket: the required standing cell or destination is outside the pocket or board. Neither original teleport is adjacent to the gate. An optimistic pre-final-chip ordinary-monster reachability model excludes the listed gate cells and both teleports; its assumptions and limits are preserved separately.
  11. A fresh ordinary push from (23,0) followed solely by the corridor trip to (31,0) and back requires at least 69 ticks: the earliest first move is one tick after the push, followed by seventeen moves four ticks apart. This bound assumes no forced-ice excursion, movement-state reset or other shortcut; it is not a universal return-time bound.
  12. The remaining question is global slipper scheduling and eventual return reachability. Native list reordering can skip or repeat block movements, so a fixed nominal opening time does not prove impossibility. No invariant covers all attainable helper oscillators, mixed normal/reversing predecessors, clone-corridor occupancy, forced-ice departures, legitimate mouse timing or returns on later block cycles. No complete winning route has been established.

Scope and limitations
  • The 53-chip fixture is a single native-verified partial route, not a winning TWS file. It replaces the older 40-chip route as the strongest constructive checkpoint; the intermediate 52-chip fixture remains available.
  • No complete impossibility argument is claimed. Bounded local searches and timing probes cover selected configurations only.
  • Native verification refers to the original Tile World MS implementation. These routes were not independently verified in CHIP.EXE or SuCC.
  • The topology and ordinary-monster models establish only their stated geometric exclusions. They do not bound every attainable global slipper schedule; the 69-tick fresh-push estimate is conditional, and the separate ice-excursion probe is TypeScript-only.
