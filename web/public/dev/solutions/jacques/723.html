Findings so far

  1. The preserved partial route is alive at tick 2136 with Chip at (30,20), 40 chips collected and 14 remaining. All 2,137 steps match between the TypeScript replay and native Tile World MS trace.
  2. The first block gate is reversible. Push the block from (20,1) onto the ice, circle underneath it, and push north at tick 578 as it returns to (21,1). It parks at (21,0). Chip can then use the lower loop through (21,2), (22,2) and (22,1) to collect (22,0) and return to the left side.
  3. The central bomb at (8,16) can be cleared. Enter the middle clone corridor, wait for a fresh block at (11,13), extract it south, push it west along row 15, and push it down into the bomb. This opens the lower rooms.
  4. The partial route also collects all sixteen chips beneath the ice-grid blocks at x=17,21,25,29 and y=16..19. Push west on a tick congruent to 2 modulo 4, then retreat east on the next eligible input poll, before the sliding block returns.
  5. An alternate early prefix collects the upper dead-end chip at (29,5) and returns to (30,8) at tick 836, before opening the central passage allows a fireball into the glider corridor. Its 837 steps also match native Tile World exactly. This alternate prefix has not been joined to the longer 40-chip route.
  6. The second upper block gate remains unresolved. Its block starts over the chip at (24,0), slides along (25..27,0), and bounces off the wall at (28,0). The route to the chip at (31,0) descends to row 1 and comes up at (29,0); the returning block closes (24,0) before the tested round trip. North-facing thin walls beneath the ice prevent the same perpendicular parking move used at the first gate. Timing experiments have not established either a successful return or an invariant ruling one out.
  7. The other remaining areas still need constructive routes, including the right-hand bomb and block room. No conclusion of impossibility follows from the remaining chips or unsuccessful local timing trials.
