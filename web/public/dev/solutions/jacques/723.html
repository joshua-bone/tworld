723. Marsupilami Block

Impossibility argument

The chip at (31,0) can be collected, but Chip cannot escape its corridor. A global scheduler resource bound returns the gate block within 70 ticks; safe escape needs at least 73. All 54 chips are required to reach an exit, so the original level is impossible in native Tile World MS.

Why completion is impossible

  1. Exactly 54 chip units exist and 54 are required. Both exits lie behind the socket at (15,28), so the chip at (31,0) is mandatory.
  2. Removing gateway (24,0) leaves a nine-cell pocket containing (31,0). Permanent walls and north thin walls exclude alternate entry, escape, and perpendicular pushes into the gate ice. No exit or teleporter lies in the pocket.
  3. Consider the last entry from (23,0) into (24,0) before collecting that chip and escaping. All earlier helper construction is allowed. The leftmost gate block needs at most five forced advances, with at most one reversal/requeue, to stop at (24,0). Other gate blocks only obstruct it sooner.
  4. Native queue reordering can delay the gate. For one target queue interval, a predecessor's first removal/requeue absorbs earlier skips and can emit one replacement skip. Silent departures delete roots; departures that clone a slider can still emit a root. New clones append behind the target.
  5. These skip dependencies form a forest. Give a predecessor cost 1 for at most 3 normal advances, cost 2 for 4, cost 3 for 5, and cost 4 for 6. Exact integer recurrence proves that sixteen target skips require at least 129 total cost, regardless of queue order or mixed phases.
  6. The board permits at most 80 short predecessors and cost 12 in each of four long ice rows: total 128. Long-row blocks cannot pass, disappear or leave their nine-cell extended row; k contributors have at most min(6,9-k) normal advances. Teleporter and collision audits exclude surviving actor overlaps that could evade this occupancy bound.
  7. Therefore each of the gate's at most two queue intervals has at most 15 skips. Five advances plus thirty skipped two-tick passes return the block to (24,0) within 70 ticks. It then stops on ordinary floor, with no later autonomous reopening and no removal possible from inside.
  8. Last entry to ordinary floor (24,0) sets HASMOVED. Keyboard and mouse input are rejected until the next four-tick boundary. Optional ice excursions cannot accelerate first pocket entry. The nineteen manual moves needed to collect (31,0) and clear west to (23,0) therefore require at least 73 ticks.
  9. Arriving merely at (24,0) is insufficient: the incoming block kills Chip before the same-tick manual west input. The universal 70-tick return is earlier than the 73-tick minimum safe escape, proving impossibility in original native Tile World MS.

Scope and limitations
  • The proof concerns the original native Tile World MS implementation and exact Jacques DAT hash. CHIP.EXE and SuCC were not independently audited.
  • The integer verifier checks the finite geometry/arithmetic certificate; the native-source and forest lemmas are written mathematical arguments, not a machine-checked exhaustive state-space proof.
  • The preserved 53-chip and 54-chip routes are nonwinning evidence. Historical timing probes and their conditional 69-tick estimate are superseded by the universal 70-versus73 argument; no winning TWS is claimed.
