978. A Bit Walkery

Under investigation

A native-verified partial replay collects 1,914 of the 1,940 required chips and remains alive at tick 9675, but its walker source is sealed in a fully occupied chip-bounded component. A separate native-verified connected route collects 1,053 chips by tick 4387 while keeping Chip and all active actors in one chip-free component. Neither route establishes a win or a complete impossibility argument.

Findings so far

  1. The source walker at (6,2) faces west on a cloner. A ball repeatedly crosses the connected red button at (23,3), filling the corridor from (2,2) to (6,2).
  2. The surrounding chips initially contain the walkers. Removing the last chip from a boundary cell admits monsters into that cell; a state can appear alive until the next monster movement phase, when Chip may be killed before being able to leave.
  3. A bounded opening search found a legal prefix that collects all 18 chips on the nine corridor-adjacent cells by tick 115 and has at least one surviving continuation. This rules out treating those cells as permanently inaccessible. It does not establish a complete solution.
  4. After clearing the 18 corridor-adjacent chips, Chip escapes to the open board by tick 143. A longer route collects 1,914 chips and is alive at (1,0) at tick 9675. Every initial-state, step, and result field matches the original native engine for this 9,676-input prefix. The corner is then obstructed by walkers, so this prefix does not demonstrate a winning continuation.
  5. An audit of both planes finds exactly one exit at (31,0), buried below a socket. There is no alternate buried-exit shortcut; the socket requires the chip counter to reach zero.
  6. Chip-barrier connectivity explains the previous bulk-route failure: at its final state, the source component has 18 cells occupied by 18 non-Chip actors, while a separate 988-cell component contains seven actors. A state-specific saturated component is not a level-wide impossibility argument.
  7. A protected setup that clears the surrounding rectangle while retaining one chip on each of the nine corridor boundary cells matches the native engine for all 244 inputs. In a separate bounded TypeScript probe, eight seeds, waits of zero through seven ticks, and reachable approach sides give 960 entry cases: 240 survive the entry window, but none has a surviving four-tick cardinal departure. Arbitrary schedules and seeds remain unexhausted.
  8. Alternating visits to adjacent chip cells and local actual-engine routing produce a different connected prefix: 1,053 chips collected by tick 4387, exact native parity for all 4,388 inputs. Its chip-barrier diagnostic has one 575-cell component containing Chip and all 289 active non-Chip actors, with 285 cells empty after counting Chip separately. This is a route-development checkpoint, not a winning replay.
