Findings so far

  1. The source walker at (6,2) faces west on a cloner. A ball repeatedly crosses the connected red button at (23,3), filling the corridor from (2,2) to (6,2).
  2. The surrounding chips initially contain the walkers. Removing the last chip from a boundary cell admits monsters into that cell; a state can appear alive until the next monster movement phase, when Chip may be killed before being able to leave.
  3. A bounded opening search found a legal prefix that collects all 18 chips on the nine corridor-adjacent cells by tick 115 and has at least one surviving continuation. This rules out treating those cells as permanently inaccessible. It does not establish a complete solution.
  4. After clearing the 18 corridor-adjacent chips, Chip escapes to the open board by tick 143. A longer route collects 1,914 chips and is alive at (1,0) at tick 9675. Every initial-state, step, and result field matches the original native engine for this 9,676-input prefix. The corner is then obstructed by walkers, so this prefix does not demonstrate a winning continuation.
